Hi, I'm Yi Fan, a final-year Mathematics and Applied Mathematics student at Xiamen University Malaysia (GPA 3.83/4.0, top 5%).

During my undergraduate years I worked across stochastic analysis, mathematical finance and robotics data, and kept coming back to one question: how do we build known structure into a model so that it is both learnable and explainable? My final-year thesis, *Malliavin Calculus for Variance-Optimal Hedging under Rough Volatility*, was graded A. It studies hedging under rough volatility, where the model carries a strong memory of its own past that generic neural networks struggle to learn from finite data. My approach was to write that known structure directly into the network, let the network learn only what remains, and prove how fast the hedging error converges. At Great Bay University's ThERA Lab I worked on autonomous ultrasound scanning, handling data processing and statistical analysis and extracting physically meaningful features from force and motion data under mechanical modeling assumptions, for a paper accepted at IROS 2026.

Beyond research, I have also tried bringing modeling into business and product problems. At the Red Bird Challenge Camp at HKUST(GZ) in August 2026, teammates from several disciplines and I started from a real need and built a smart lunch box for people with type 2 diabetes; I was solely responsible for its glucose-response model. On a visit to a low-altitude safety company during the camp, an engineer told me something I still remember: on a production line, the models people trust are the ones where every number can be explained. Earlier, at EY's 2024 Gorton ESG Challenge, I led industry research and route comparison for the Environment Module of a "low-altitude economy + ESG" brief; our team placed in the national top four and won the Northeast China region.
