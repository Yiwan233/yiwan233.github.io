During my undergraduate years I deliberately explored widely, across stochastic analysis, mathematical finance and robotics data. ThERA Lab was where I first did engineering research, extracting physically meaningful features from force and motion data under mechanical modeling assumptions. I also tried bringing modeling into business and product problems. At the Red Bird Challenge Camp at HKUST(GZ) in August 2026, teammates from several disciplines and I started from a real need and built a smart lunch box for people with type 2 diabetes; I was solely responsible for its glucose-response model. On a visit to a low-altitude safety company during the camp, an engineer told me something I still remember: on a production line, the models people trust are the ones where every number can be explained. Earlier, at EY's 2024 Gorton ESG Challenge, I led the Environment Module of a "low-altitude economy + ESG" brief; our team placed in the national top four and won the Northeast China region.

I compete regularly in mathematical modeling contests, with a Meritorious Winner award at MCM/ICM and a national second prize at the China Undergraduate Mathematical Contest in Modeling. After my first MCM I formed a three-person study group to teach each other modeling; with no existing club to join, we ran it for half a year with support from the department's student union before formally founding **MFA Round Table** (模法师圆桌), which later hosted an introductory modeling lecture for 170+ people.

Looking back, what I have converged on is a method, not an application: stochastic modeling and uncertainty quantification. The application domain is open, and I'm happy to work on problems beyond the areas above if they involve stochastic modeling or UQ. **If you are interested in research collaboration, co-authored papers or cross-disciplinary projects, I would be glad to hear from you through the Contact page.**
